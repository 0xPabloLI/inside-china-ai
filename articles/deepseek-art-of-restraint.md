# The Art of Restraint: DeepSeek's Vision-Driven Strategy for AGI

> **Source**: This article is based on a transcript from Liang Wenfeng's investor meeting on May 20, 2026 ([full transcript PDF](https://inside-china-ai.com/posts/deepseek-art-of-restraint/)). The recording was transcribed by AI and spans approximately 3 hours and 44 minutes.

---

## Vision Over KPIs

DeepSeek started with an unusual premise: a group of ordinary people united by a vision, not by stock options or KPIs.

"I never thought about how much money we'd make, going public, or anything like that," CEO Liang Wenfeng told investors. "We started with a great kindness toward the world. We wanted to do something useful for humanity — beyond money."

This wasn't empty rhetoric. When DeepSeek's V3.2 Flash model generated unexpected revenue, Liang made a controversial decision: cut the price to one-fourth of the original.

"The team cheered when I lowered the price," Liang said. "That's the purpose of all our work — to make it affordable and high-quality. That's what makes us happy."

DeepSeek has no organization chart, no performance reviews, no quotas. Instead, it relies entirely on a shared vision. "Vision isn't a slogan on the wall," Liang explained. "Vision is how you actually do things, not what you say."

This unconventional approach traces back to Liang's early management hero: Jack Welch, the former GE CEO. "Most of what he said turned out wrong," Liang admitted, "but he got one thing right: a company's vision is its most important asset."

---

## The Paradox of Open Source

DeepSeek is among the most aggressive open-source AI companies. Their reasoning: AI is too big for anyone to own.

"In history, a software company might have a market of tens of billions of dollars. Without open source, it's gone — maybe tens or hundreds of millions left," Liang said. "But AI is different. It could eventually occupy 10% of global GDP. You can't monopolize that. If you try, history will abandon you."

This isn't idealism; it's strategy. By keeping profits modest and models open, DeepSeek increases its chances of building AGI.

Liang compared this to Zhipu AI (GLM), which also open-sources models. "Their open source feels forced," Liang said. "For us, it's intentional. We believe the vision itself requires open source."

## The 10-Month Rule

DeepSeek's API pricing follows a simple formula: recover hardware costs in 10 months.

"That's a reasonable profit," Liang said. "Financially, we amortize over 3-5 years. Commercially, we say 10 months is enough."

At sixfold profit, open source doesn't hurt revenue. Third parties can't match DeepSeek's deployment costs. "If we wanted 100x profit, open source would be a problem," Liang admitted. "At 6x, it doesn't matter."

The pricing has no elasticity. "I could double the price and demand wouldn't change much," Liang said. "Everyone's already using it. Lowering it further doesn't add happiness or value."

## Restraint as Strategy

DeepSeek practices restraint in multiple dimensions: pricing (10-month recovery, not profit maximization), user acquisition (last spring, when users flooded in, they didn't monetize or lock them in), and competition (they avoid fighting for short-term wins).

"Why fight over sesame seeds when there's a watermelon ahead?" Liang asked. "Last year's C-end DAUs were trivial. AGI is the real opportunity."

This restraint creates strategic advantages. By not fighting for today's battles, DeepSeek reserves energy for tomorrow's war.

"Strange how the things you chase hardest escape you," Liang observed. "The things you don't obsess over often come easily."

He calls this "dimensionality reduction attack": pursuing AGI at a high level, then treating commercial products as byproducts.

---

## The AGI Roadmap

DeepSeek views AGI development as a series of steps, each building on the last:

| Phase | Technology | What It Solved | What's Next |
| ----- | ---------- | -------------- | ----------- |
| **Past** | Language Models | Basic understanding | Chain-of-Thought |
| **2024** | Chain-of-Thought (CoT) | Self-reasoning capability | Agents |
| **2025** | Agents | Multi-task orchestration | Continuous Learning |
| **Future** | Continuous Learning | In-context knowledge retention | Self-Iteration Singularity |
| **Beyond** | Self-Iteration | AI develops next AI versions | Embodied AI |

"We're at the Agent step now," Liang said. "The bottleneck we see is continuous learning — enabling models to learn incrementally, like humans."

After that lies the "singularity": when AI can develop its own next versions. Then embodied AI, robots entering the physical world to handle daily tasks and elder care.

"We see this as the easiest path," Liang said. "Each step builds on the last. We don't need overtime. If we did it in reverse order — embodied AI first — it would be brutal work."

---

## Commercialization as Byproduct

DeepSeek's C-end users and B-end customers are both byproducts of their AGI pursuit.

"We're not doing C-end or B-end for their own sake," Liang said. "We're doing AGI. These just happen to be useful outputs along the way."

This contrasts with competitors whose vision is "serve users well." They excel at product, customer service, traffic. DeepSeek excels at technology.

"The model is the most important thing right now," Liang said. "Everything else follows."

This explains DeepSeek's trajectory. When they went viral last spring, it wasn't in their playbook. They just wanted to build good technology. Their AGI-focused organization gave them advantages in talent and culture that product-first companies lacked.

"People fought head over blood for C-end traffic," Liang recalled. "The person who didn't fight walked away with it. That proves the logic works."

---

## The One Core Interest

DeepSeek has only one core interest that cannot be compromised: **team stability**.

"As long as the team stays, we'll build AGI. It's that simple," Liang said. "Money isn't an issue. Resources aren't an issue. Those are easy."

This is both their greatest strength and greatest risk. Recent funding eased the pressure; key employees received substantial equity. When core veterans stay, others are less likely to leave.

"People join because they want to be part of building AGI," Liang said. "That's attractive."

DeepSeek's historically low employee turnover supports this. But maintaining it remains their "only challenge."

"Everything else is a matter of time," Liang said. "At worst, we're six months to a year late. We won't fail."

---

## My Take: Why This Matters

DeepSeek's strategy challenges conventional AI startup wisdom:

1. **Restraint isn't weakness**: it's a deliberate trade-off for long-term advantage
2. **Open source as moat**: by not trying to own everything, they reduce resistance to adoption
3. **Vision as coordination mechanism**: replaces org charts and KPIs with shared purpose
4. **Commercialization as fuel, not destination**: funds the real mission (AGI) without distraction

The 10-month pricing rule sends a powerful signal: "We're not here to maximize profit. We're here to maximize reach and impact." This attracts talent aligned with that mission and creates differentiation from competitors focused on ARR.

The AGI roadmap shows clear thinking about technical dependencies. By sequencing CoT → Agents → Continuous Learning → Self-Iteration → Embodied AI, each step builds on the last. This isn't random; it's deliberate, achievable, and doesn't require overtime.

Most telling is the "team stability as core interest" philosophy. In an industry where bidding wars and poaching are common, DeepSeek takes a different approach: create a mission so compelling people don't want to leave.

The question is whether this unconventional approach can withstand market pressures. As competitors with war chests and product focus attack, will DeepSeek's restraint look strategic or naive?

Time will tell. But for now, they're executing on a vision that's both ambitious and surprisingly disciplined.
