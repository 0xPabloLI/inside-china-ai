**This caption set is the spine of the video package.** Unitree qualifies its own claims,
on screen, in its own video:

1. The description says "maximum endurance of approximately 8 hours." The on-screen caption
   adds the qualifier the description omits: **"Standing."** Standing still for 8 hours and
   working for 8 hours are different claims, and the company knows which one it filmed.
2. The knife and car scenes are labelled **"Security Demonstration"** and carry
   **"Real Footage of Props for Reference Only."** Frame inspection at 52.5 s confirms the
   "victim" under the car is a mannequin, not a person.
3. The video ends by telling buyers to **"thoroughly understand the limitations of humanoid
   robots before making a purchase."** The company's own closing line is a caution.
4. "Real Footage Throughout No Speed-Up" defends the *motion's* authenticity while the other
   captions bound the *interpretation*. Both are true at once, and that tension is the story.

## The second axis: the real machine

On 2026-10-22, one day after the launch, an H2-X fell during a side-flip demonstration at
Unitree's own partner conference. Its lower mechanical leg detached at the joint. Video was
captured by audience members. **No official technical explanation has been published.**

Reported reactions worth knowing: some observers noted the body stayed intact after losing
the leg; others read it as proof that real motion is happening; several drew the line at
"doing a backflip is not the same as being able to do the job."

This is a factual counterweight to the polished launch film, and it happened to the same
product, in the same week, in front of Unitree's own partners. It is verified by multiple
independent outlets and the audience footage. **It is deliberately kept out of the video
package** — see "Angle for the video package" for why.

## Angle for the video package

Settled by the user on 2026-10-24, and it overrides the earlier proposal below.

**The angle: report the capabilities straight.** The video shows what Unitree's own 61 second
film shows — a guard humanoid lifting the front of a car, intercepting a knife-wielding
mannequin at a night villa, pushing a dummy clear of an oncoming car, dropping off a 4 meter
platform, and standing for roughly 8 hours — and lets Unitree's own burned-in English
captions carry the claims. The captions are the evidence, so several are kept legible in
frame.

The user's words: 「我希望这个新闻就报道宇树那些fancy的安保机器人内容就行。不用去刻意制造你上面提的这种矛盾。」 Footage
reuse was also the user's call: 「全片复用多个片段，含片内英文字幕原样入镜（字幕即证据）」.

The one honest qualifier that stays in the package is Unitree's own closing card (extreme
testing, no imitation, 2–3 m safe distance, features vary by model) — it is a caveat the
company wrote, not a contradiction the package manufactured.

**Rejected: the Contradiction (P2) angle.** The earlier proposal was to play the polished film
against the 2026-10-22 leg detachment and the 2026-10-20 overvaluation filing. That is what
维科网 / OFweek's self-media piece 《24小时内，王兴兴先泼冷水，再让机器人徒手抬车》 builds on, and the
user explicitly turned it down. Both facts stay documented above for the record; neither is
used as narrative.

**Rejected: "Unitree's robot can lift a car" as a standalone claim.** The car lift is shown,
but framed as something in Unitree's film, not as an independently verified capability — the
props disclaimer governs it.
